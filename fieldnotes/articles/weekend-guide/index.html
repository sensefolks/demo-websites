The weekend guide · No. 024

A weekend with no particular hurry.

Two days, a small bag, and a gentler way to get away. A field guide to leaving room for the unexpected.

6 min readSlow travel

Words by Rowan Ellis · The Fieldnotes editors

The first thing we did was take something off the itinerary. The second was sit down for a coffee. It turned out to be an excellent start.

There is a particular kind of tiredness that comes from treating a weekend like a project. The early train, the perfect photograph, the three places you must eat. You return with a full camera roll and the distinct feeling you could use another day off.

This is an invitation to try something smaller. Choose one landscape you like. Find a comfortable base near it. Leave the rest of the page blank. A good weekend does not need to be a story worth telling at dinner. It can just be a weekend you enjoyed being in.

Friday: arrive, then stop.

We like to arrive with enough daylight to get our bearings. Not to conquer the neighbourhood, just to find the essentials: somewhere for breakfast, the start of a path, a bench with a decent view. Then we put the bags down properly and leave them alone.

A simple supper is plenty. Read the local noticeboard. Ask someone where they walk their dog. Take the recommendation that sounds least like an attraction. The small places have a way of becoming the ones you remember.

“Make one good plan. Leave space around it.”

Before bed, look out of the window. Make a loose plan for the weather you actually have, rather than the weather you had in mind. A grey morning under trees can be every bit as lovely as a bright one on the hill.

Saturday: the quiet way through the trees.

Our favourite walks begin with no announcement. A gap in a hedge. A small wooden sign. The tarmac becoming gravel underfoot. For this imaginary weekend, we follow a sheltered woodland loop with enough places to pause that the distance stops mattering.

Walk at the pace of the most interesting thing you can see. Notice how the light changes at a clearing, how the air cools beside water, how a familiar conversation sounds different when you are looking in the same direction. You do not need to identify every tree to enjoy being among them.

At lunchtime we find somewhere comfortable to sit. Bread, a little cheese, an apple. There is no performance in it. Just enough food to turn a pause into an occasion. We take everything we brought back with us, including the crumbs in the bottom of the bag.

The afternoon stays open. Perhaps a second loop, perhaps a book and an early return. Taking the shorter path is allowed. So is sitting on the same bench twice. The aim is to come home with energy left over.

Sunday: follow the coast, forget the clock.

If there is water nearby, we start there. A riverbank does the job as well as the sea. Fill a flask, bring an extra layer, and head out before the day has acquired too many decisions. You are looking for somewhere to watch the world wake up.

We have a small ritual: the first ten minutes are for looking. No headphones, no photographs, no choosing the next stop. Just the changing surface of the water and the pleasantly ordinary business of people beginning their day.

Later, write down one thing you would otherwise forget. Not the name of the place; you can find that again. The sound of loose rigging. A dog determined to carry a branch twice its size. The coffee that was somehow better outdoors. A single sentence is enough of a fieldnote.

A small bag, thoughtfully packed.

The list is shorter than you think: comfortable walking shoes, a weatherproof layer, water, a simple lunch, a charged phone, and any everyday essentials you need. Add a notebook if you like making marks on paper. Leave space for a book you may not read.

Before heading out, check the local forecast, access notices, and the route itself. Our weekend is a fictional sketch, so use a real local map to shape your own version. A little preparation makes it easier to relax into the day.

And one final thing: give yourself an easy journey home. There is no prize for filling the last available hour. Leave while you still feel like staying. Unpack the bag. Put the kettle on. Let the weekend arrive home with you.
